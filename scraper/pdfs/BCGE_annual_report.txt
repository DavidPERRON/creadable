Message from our Chair:
We can look to the future
with confidence
In 2025 we delivered another solid year, with a net profit similar to that in 2024. We
didn’t waiver in fulfilling our mission as Geneva’s cantonal bank, despite the many
uncertainties weighing on our country, its economy and its people.
Our business volumes grew at an encouraging pace. We saw expansion in both our
loan book and customer deposits, again testifying to the strengths of our diversified
business model, perfectly suited to respond to our clients’ needs.
Our balance sheet is healthy and we’re continuing to shore up our capital, thus
securing a stable, lasting future for our Bank. I would also mention that the growth
Manuel Leuthold
in business volumes came without any additional risk-taking or any compromise on
our profit margins. At the Board of Directors, we continually and carefully monitor
the numerous risks our Bank is exposed to.
Strong headwinds in 2025
2025 was not an easy year for the Swiss economy. Our companies faced additional pressure from the
US tariffs on Swiss goods and the Swiss franc’s sharp appreciation against the dollar. But our economy’s
versatility and capacity to adapt once again enabled Switzerland to overcome these obstacles.
On the other hand, 2025 was a great year for Swiss real estate and especially residential property. The
market was supported by persistently strong demand and low interest rates, all of which translated into
a marginal default rate on our loan book.
Financial markets performed well in 2025; investors appeared unfazed by the turbulent geopolitical
climate. This lifted the volume of client assets we manage and therefore our fee income.
New legal framework
The cantonal law governing our Bank (LBCGe) was revised in the wake of the 2023 update to the Swiss
federal law on public limited companies. The changes to the LBCGe were designed to align our Bank
with the new requirements under the Swiss Code of Obligations, and have resulted in a more modern
governance system, improved transparency and greater agility, as reflected in the stock split we carried
out in October 2025.
The revised LBCGe was approved unanimously by the Grand Council of Geneva and went into force on
1 September 2025. But before that, we updated our Bank’s articles of association in line with the revised
LBCGe; the new articles of association were approved at the general shareholders’ meeting on 29 April
2025 and then ratified by the Grand Council on 22 May 2025. They took effect in October 2025.
Bottom line that benefits all our stakeholders
Our 2025 net profit will benefit everyone who played a role. At the upcoming general shareholders’
meeting, the Board of Directors will propose a dividend of CHF 0.70 per share, up 7.7% from the prior
year. We also intend to further build up our equity in order to support the Bank’s future growth. The
value we created in 2025 will be shared with our shareholders, our employees, our clients and our
broader ecosystem.
Acknowledgements
On behalf of the Board of Directors, I would like to thank everyone who has contributed to the smooth
operation of BCGE Group. Our thanks go in particular to our shareholders for their unfailing support, to
our customers for their trust and loyalty, and to our employees who work tirelessly to serve those
customers. We also express our gratitude to the Executive Board, steered by the expert hands of our
CEO Nicolas Krügel, and to our supervisory authorities for their continual, constructive guidance. On a
personal level, I would like to thank my fellow Board members for their diligence and dedication.
Our Bank can look to the future with confidence.

Message from our CEO:
A year of growth, innovation
and diversification

Nicolas Krügel

It took nerves of steel to navigate 2025. Switzerland – like all of the United States’
trading partners – grappled with Washington’s numerous tariff announcements, and
the swift drop in the US dollar weighed on Swiss exports. Economies in southern
Europe picked up during the year, but political instability in France and structural headwinds in German manufacturing resulted in weaker demand across the continent. In
Switzerland, the strong franc shaped currency movements while interest rates hit rockbottom. In this uncertain environment, our advisors stood by our clients, providing
them with clear insights, concrete facts and a sound basis for decision making. We
partnered with our clients to help them reach their goals.

Gaining market share
This approach was instrumental to our business development in 2025. We grew significantly and expanded
our market share, continuing the trend from 2024. Our loan book added 3.9%, our assets under
management increased by CHF 3.4 billion, and we now serve and advise 258,632 customers.
Solid financial performance with a healthy risk profile
Our net interest income was impacted by the return of 0% interest rates in Switzerland. We were able to
offset much of that impact by working strategically on our margins and growing our fee and commission
income. We also expanded our capacity to serve wealthy clients by rolling out new, high-quality discretionary asset-management services, which should position our parent company as one of the region’s
leading private banks.
Firm cost control kept our cost/income ratio at 53.7% and enabled us to achieve significant productivity
gains. We ended the year with an ROE of 9.1%, underscoring our ability to create shareholder value in line
with other banks of a comparable size and complexity.
Our liquidity ratios sit well above the regulatory requirements. Our loan book is financed entirely by
customer deposits and has a relatively low default rate. Our total equity ratio remains high, notwithstanding
the implementation of the final Basel III standards, the sustained growth in our balance sheet and the reimbursement of our Additional Tier 1 (AT1) capital loan. All this gives us a healthy risk profile, as confirmed by
S&P’s decision to maintain our AA– rating.
Further innovating and modernising our Bank
2025 was a year of innovation on all fronts. In March, we became the first category 3 cantonal bank to issue
a euro-denominated bond in the European market. The issue was six times oversubscribed, illustrating
how we’re able to diversify our sources of financing. Also in the spring, we introduced our Stu card for
young people, which has proven popular. In the fourth quarter we unveiled our Optimum range of discretionary asset-management solutions, which are being implemented by the investment team headed by our
new CIO. Our new Boost and Boost+ loyalty programmes let savers earn up to 1.4% additional interest on
their accounts – it’s the first programme of its kind on the market. And we launched two new Synchrony
Equantia funds at the end of the year; these actively managed funds use an AI-driven process to select the
investment universe, with many institutional investors showing an interest.
We acquired a new building in Vernier in December. It will allow us to both shrink our carbon footprint –
thanks to its SNBS certification – and give our employees more modern workspaces linked to the main
public transport lines. The new building will support our growth going forward.
In 2025, we continued our healthy growth trajectory across our franchise. Our diversified business model
enabled us to stand firm in a turbulent environment and create value for all our stakeholders. This success
was made possible through the hard work of our employees, who stood by our clients and delivered
proactive responses. My sincerest thanks for your efforts. I would also like to thank our Board of Directors,
shareholders and clients for their trust in us, which is key to our Group’s future success.

About BCGE Group
A universal bank firmly rooted
in Geneva since 1816
At BCGE, we take pride in being a bank that is reliable, sound, agile
and performance-driven. Since 1816, we have continued to evolve while
staying true to our traditions and cultivating close relationships with
our clients.
Putting families first
Banque Cantonale de Genève (BCGE) was born 210 years ago at a time when only the most
privileged members of society had access to banking and financial services. It was created by
Genevans to help families manage and protect their finances by encouraging them to put
aside savings.
A universal bank
We’ve built upon that rich history to evolve and grow over time – for instance, we added advisory
services and loans for business owners. Today, we are a universal bank that offers a wide range
of banking and financial services to ensure that all of our clients’ needs are met.
Anchored in Geneva
We’re based in Geneva, a global hub for financial expertise. As the partner of choice for Geneva’s
businesses, we contribute to bolstering the local and national economy. And like Geneva, we
have a broad international reach, thanks to our role as a leading centre for global trade.
Our subsidiaries
Banque Cantonale de Genève France SA provides loans for businesses and real estate professionals, as well as wealth management services for private clients. Its headquarters are in Lyon
and it also has branches in Annecy and Paris.
Capital Transmission SA is a private equity investment firm that aims to create long-term value
for the businesses it invests in. It invests its own capital in the transmission and expansion of
businesses, both in the greater Geneva region and in other parts of Switzerland and Europe.
Dimension SA specialises in the sales, acquisition and valuation of businesses. It supports
business owners by helping them enhance the value of their companies and carry out mergers
and acquisitions.
Mont-Fort Funds SA provides representation for foreign investment funds in Switzerland,
diversifying BCGE Groups’ paying agent services and strengthening its position on the asset
servicing market.
Loyal Finance AG is a fixed income specialist that works with institutional investors in the Swiss
German region, enhancing BCGE Group’s expertise in managing fixed-income instruments and
broadening our services and client base within Switzerland.

We strive to fulfil the dreams of others.
At BCGE, we strive for authenticity, courage and fairness in all our endeavours as we
seek to create value for our clients, shareholders and employees. This means taking
measured risks in order to seek appropriate returns, while complying with the regulations
that apply in the jurisdictions in which we operate. We then proactively manage these risks.
This provides us with long-term free cash flows to generate returns on invested capital,
unpderin organic growth and invest in high-performing technology.

Each day, we are driven by our three key
values: authenticity, courage and fairness.
When building our teams we look for creativity, a desire to excel and an enterprising
mindset. We bring together people with diverse but complementary backgrounds. Our
teams are made up of banking experts, versatile employees and specialists in financial
services. Our teams are spread across all of the Bank’s business lines and strategic activities,
but ultimately, we’re all working towards the same objective: to meet our clients’ needs by
offering them a competitive range of services.

Corporate culture
Authenticity
• At BCGE, creating space for transparent and constructive dialogue is a cornerstone of our corporate
culture.
• We encourage close contact and direct exchanges between our managers and our employees to
promote innovation and a solution-driven approach.
• We’re people-oriented. We want to simplify the day-to-day lives of our clients and closely engage with
them so that we can offer them truly personalised services.

Courage
• We value the courage of employees who are driven by their convictions, are able to adapt and have an
enterprising streak – qualities that allow us to evolve.
• We actively take on the challenge of ensuring that the knowledge and skills required for highly
specialised roles are passed down by carefully managing key positions and key employees.
• As a universal bank, we stand out for our customised, highly specialised banking and financial services.

Fairness
• We encourage our employees to further develop their professional skills so that each of them has the
opportunity to shine.
• We offer them advanced, continuous training in all areas of banking and finance so that they can
prepare for and adapt to changes in the sector.
• We’re constantly evolving. We seek to mobilise our expertise and look to the future so that we can
continue to meet our clients’ needs over the long term. When our employees thrive, BCGE thrives!

1. Group structure and ownership
1.1

Group structure

1.1.1 Operating structure
• Banque Cantonale de Genève (hereafter BCGE) is a limited company established by public law
according to Article 763 of the Swiss Code of Obligations (CO). It has the status of a cantonal
bank as defined by the Swiss Federal Act on Banks and Savings Banks (LB).
• The Bank was registered in the Geneva Trade Register on 17 December 1993 and conducts
its business under the registered name “Banque Cantonale de Genève SA”.
• The Bank’s headquarters and governing bodies are located in Geneva.
• BCGE operates branches and agencies, and representative offices abroad.
• The profile of the Group and its subsidiaries can be found on pages 6 to 11 of this report.
• The scope of consolidation is presented in sections 1.1.3 and 2 of this report.
• BCGE is made up of six divisions headed by members of the Executive Board.
• The CEO and his staff oversee coordination and collaboration across all divisions using a
matrix structure.
• They are supported by various strategic committees that have been assigned key responsibilities and controls by the Executive Board.
• The operating structure of BCGE as at 1 March 2026 is shown on pages 12 and 13 of this
report.

2.6 Restrictions on transfers and registration of nominees

2.6.1 Restrictions on transfers
Article 3, paragraph 9, of the Bank’s Articles of Association contains an anti-fiduciary clause
providing that buyers are only included in the shareholder register with voting rights to the
extent that they expressly declare that they acquired the shares in their own name and for
their own account.
• Article 189, paragraph 2, of the Constitution of the Republic and Geneva Canton also provides
that the Canton and the municipalities must hold the majority of the voting rights attached to
the Bank’s share capital.
• Article 7, paragraph 3 of the Act of the Banque Cantonale de Genève (Loi sur la Banque cantonale de Genève (LBCGe) in force since 1 April 2016) specifies that the Canton, the City of Geneva
and the other municipalities represented by the Association of Geneva Municipalities
(Association des communes genevoises, ACG) must enter into a shareholder agreement
governing the minimum number of shares that must be held by each public authority.
•

2.6.2 Reasons for granting exceptions
No exceptions are granted.
2.6.3 Registration of nominees
As at 31 December 2025, there is no statutory or regulatory provision permitting nominees to
be included in the shareholder register.
2.6.4 Procedure
Article 3, paragraph 10 of the Bank’s Articles of Association stipulates that the Board of Directors
may specify the terms and adopt the regulations required for the implementation of this Article.
Such regulations must set out the cases in which the Board or any committee appointed by it
may authorise exceptions to the restrictions to registration or to the regulation on fiduciaries or
nominees. No such regulations have been promulgated to date.

2.7 Convertible bonds and options

BCGE has not issued any convertible bonds or options.

3. Board of Directors
3.1 Members

The Board of Directors consists of 11 non-executive members, 10 of whom are independent
within the meaning of FINMA Circular 2017/1 Corporate governance – banks.
No director:
• is or has, in the previous two years, been employed in another function within the institution.
• has held the position, in the previous two years, of the Bank’s lead auditor within the auditing
firm.
• has any commercial links with the Bank which, due to their nature and scope, would lead to
conflicts of interest.
• has a qualified holding (within the meaning of Article 3, paragraph 2, letter c-bis of the Banking
Act) in the Bank or represents any such qualified holder.
• holds a full-time position in a cantonal or municipal executive body.
• is part of the executive board, operational management or audit function of any other bank.
• is a member of the board of directors of another bank whose geographical and sectoral
scopes of activity are in direct competition with the Bank.
• meets the limits on the number of mandates set out in section 3.3 below.
No board member appointed by the Canton or municipalities:
• receives any instructions from their appointing body regarding their activity as a member of
the Board of Directors.
• belongs to the cantonal government or administration, or to that of another municipality, or to
another cantonal or municipal public-sector entity, with the exception of a director who is
mayor of a municipality with fewer than 1,800 inhabitants.

3.3 Number of functions accepted

On 29 April 2025, the general meeting of shareholders approved the amendment of the Bank’s
Articles of Association. The amended Articles of Association provide that no member of the
Board of Directors may hold more than nine additional appointments in companies pursuing an
economic objective, of which no more than four may be in listed companies. Each of these
mandates must be approved by the Board of Directors.
In this context, “mandates” are understood to be the mandates of members of the board of
directors or management, or similar positions, within other companies pursuing an economic
objective. Mandates in several legal entities under joint control, for example within a group of
companies, are counted as a single mandate. No member of the Board of Directors may hold
more than ten such mandates per group of companies.
However, the above limits do not apply to the following mandates
• Mandates in companies under the control of the Bank
• Mandates exercised by a Board member at the request of the Bank or at the request of
companies under its control; no Board member may hold more than five such mandates.
No Board member may hold more than five such mandates.
• Mandates held in associations, charities, foundations, trusts and employee pension funds;
no Board member may hold more than five such mandates. Board members may not perform
any other activities within the Bank.
In addition, the Articles of Association provide for the following limits to the functions
that may be performed by Board members:
• Board members may not perform any other activities within the Bank.
• Board members may not hold a full-time position in a cantonal or municipal executive body;
they may not belong to the management, operational management or auditing body of
another bank. They may, however, be a board member of another bank provided that the
geographic and industrial scopes of activity of this other mandate are not in direct competition with the Bank and that they remain sufficiently available to carry out their mandate with
the Bank.

3.4 Election and duration of mandates

The 11 directors are elected or appointed as follows:
Five members appointed by the State Council of Geneva Canton
• Two members appointed by the City of Geneva;
• One member appointed by the other Geneva municipalities;
• Three members individually elected by the general meeting of shareholders; the public
authorities do not have the right to take part in these elections and exercise the voting rights
attached to the shares they are required to hold pursuant to the LBCGe and the Articles of
Association.
•

Directors are appointed for a term of four years. Members may be appointed for a maximum of
12 years. If they take up their duties partway through a term of office, the period still counts as
full four-year term.

The Board of Directors meets as often as business requires, but at least ten times a year

Decisions by the Board of Directors require the presence of the majority of its members.

3.6 Powers of the Board of Directors and the Executive Board

The competences and duties of the Board of Directors are defined in Article 17 of the Bank’s
Articles of Association and in Article 12 of the LBCGe. Article 7 of the Bank’s management and
organisational regulations additionally stipulates that the Board of Directors shall decide on:
1. strategic and financial plans and, when proposed by the Executive Board and notified by
the Risk and Strategy Committee, the risk management framework. The Board of Directors
is responsible for implementing and monitoring effective risk management and steering the
Bank’s overall risk portfolio.
2. an organisational structure aligned with the Bank’s needs.
3. an annual budget put forward by the Executive Board.
4. approval, further to a proposal of the Audit Committee, of a three-year plan of the tasks to
be perfomed by Internal Audit to ensure all necessary oversight.
5. an overall framework for limiting risk;
6. allocation of key credit risk parameters, as well as granting of loans within its mandate.
7. risk-related policies, particularly in relation to credit risks, interest rate risks, country risks
and insurance risks (based on a proposal by the Executive Board). The Board of Directors
reviews these policies as required.
8. information required in the area of risk management.
9. the strategic framework governing the Executive Board’s role in setting lending rates and
savings deposit rates.
10. the strategic framework governing the Executive Board’s role in relation to cash management, foreign exchange transactions, securities investments and holdings, and other stocks.
11. the Bank’s policy regarding the property portfolio.
12. approval of any permanent acquisition or disposal of investments.
13. acquisition and disposal of any capital goods used by the Bank, investments held as financial fixed assets, and properties, unless the Executive Board’s powers under Article 24 (3) of
these regulations stipulate otherwise.
14. write-off of receivables, compliance with out-of-court settlements, subordinations or transfers of receivables if the amount is higher than CHF 2,000,000 or the commitments were
the subject of a decision by the Board of Directors.
15. the appointment of two directors from within its ranks as members of the Control
Committee and appointment of its Chair.
16. the appointment of members of the Executive Board.
17. the appointment of the Chief Risk Officer.
18. the appointment of senior managers and their deputies.
19. policies relating to employees, remuneration and social security.

Remuneration policy
The regulations relating to employee remuneration fall under the authority of the Board of
Directors. The regulations applicable to the period under review were approved by the Board on
11 November 2015 and last amended on 25 September 2025.
Employee remuneration includes fixed remuneration, variable remuneration (which is not
systematically granted) and share-ownership plans. Fixed remuneration accounts for a major
share of employee remuneration. It is assessed on the basis of various criteria including benchmarking, market demand and competition. Each year, the Board of Directors decides on any
changes to fixed pay.
The overall amount of variable remuneration is decided each year by the Board of Directors
when year-end accounts are finalised. It includes individual variable remuneration and,
potentially, also an additional discretionary bonus if the results for the current year have
improved markedly compared with the previous three years.
Employees may receive a portion of variable pay as shares, up to 50% in the case of additional
discretionary bonuses. These shares are subject to a lock-up period of five to ten years, depending on what the employee chooses.
For some of our front office roles – including Wealth Management, Trade Finance, lending and
retail banking – variable pay is determined by remuneration models that are partially or fully
based on quantitative performance metrics. The models were developed on the basis of the
following main parameters:
• protection of clients’ interests
• recognition of individual or collective performance
• no incentive is provided to take excessive risks
• administrative rigour and alignment between variable remuneration and the Bank’s revenues.
The pay of employees in audit functions does not directly depend on the business performance
of the entities they audit.
No external consultants were engaged in the implementation of the remuneration policies for
the reporting year.

Share-ownership plans

All the Bank’s employees can choose from several share-ownership plans that enable them to
align their personal financial interests with those of the Bank. Under these plans, BCGE shares
are either granted free of charge or subject to conditions, with a lock-up period of five to ten
years, as chosen by the employee for certain plans. If a share-ownership plan requires the
employee to purchase shares, the purchased shares are subject to the same lock-up period as
the shares received free of charge.
The table on the following page and in section 4.22 of the parent company financial statements
explain the lock-up periods applicable to Bank shares acquired by parent company employees
(through share-ownership plans) and the associated tax conditions.

1.2 Executive Board

1.2.1 General principles
The remuneration paid to members of the Executive Board is intended to ensure that the Bank
remains competitive in a highly developed financial centre and is able to attract and retain the
best talents.
Pay takes into account individual responsibilities, performance evaluations and market realities.
Members of the Executive Board receive fixed and variable remuneration. They do not receive
commissions for new mortgage-lending or wealth-management business, unlike other Bank
employees.
In addition, remuneration received by members of the Executive Board in the course of their
duties on other boards of directors is passed on to the Bank in its entirety.
1.2.2 Fixed remuneration
Members of the Executive Board receive fixed remuneration for their basic duties and
responsibilities.
When a Board member is appointed, their initial fixed pay is based primarily on the typical salaries paid for comparable positions in the banking sector.
Following that, fixed pay is adjusted on the basis of changes in their responsibilities and performance, in accordance with the same rules applicable to all Bank employees.
1.2.3 Variable remuneration
Members of the Executive Board receive variable remuneration to reward individual or collective
performance over and above their standard duties.
The total amount paid out to the Executive Board as variable remuneration is determined by the
Board of Directors. The Board of Directors makes its decision based on the Bank’s profitability,
productivity, competitiveness and risk control.
This sum is then divided among the individual members of the Executive Board based on the
degree to which they achieved their specific objectives and how well their division performed.
In the event that the Bank’s performance is exceptionally strong, the Executive Board receives an
additional discretionary bonus, 50% of which is paid in the form of Bank shares subject to a fiveyear lock-up period. This bonus is awarded if the operating profit for the year under review is
higher than the average result of the two best years out of the three previous years. It was not
paid out in 2025.
Members of the Executive Board’s total pay may therefore fluctuate from one year to the next
depending on whether or not the additional discretionary bonus is paid out.

6. Shareholders’ participation rights
6.1 Limitation and representation of voting rights
6.1.1 Statutory provisions
Pursuant to Article 11, paragraph 1 of the Articles of Association, when electing members of
the Board of Directors representing shareholders other than the public authorities and the
member of the Appointments and Remuneration Committee chosen from among these
members, the public authorities do not have the right to take part in these elections and
exercise the voting rights attached to the shares they are required to hold pursuant to the
Bank’s Articles of Association.
There are no other limitations on shareholders’ voting rights nor are there any provisions in
the Articles of Association governing the granting of exceptions.
6.1.2 Additional information to be provided for issuers not subject to the provisions of
joint-stock company law pursuant to Article 620 – 762 CO
There are no limitations on voting rights or clauses governing the granting of exceptions for
institutional representatives. No exceptions were actually granted during the year under review.

6.1.3 to 6.1.6
Not applicable.
6.2 Quorum

The general meeting of shareholders shall be duly constituted irrespective of the number of
shares represented. Decisions and elections are by absolute majority of the votes attached to
the shares represented. The following decisions require a majority of at least two-thirds of
votes: the adoption or amendment of the Articles of Association; limitation or abolition of preferential subscription rights; delisting of the Bank’s equity securities; notice of the merger, splitting or transformation of the Bank, as well as its dissolution.
• For elections, if a second round of voting is required, a simple majority is sufficient. In the event
of a tie, the Chair of the general meeting of shareholders shall have the deciding vote. Elections
are held by secret ballot. At the request of 30% of the votes represented, other decisions may
also be made by secret ballot.
•

6.3 Convening of the general meeting of shareholders

The Annual general meeting of shareholders is held once a year within six months of the end
of the financial year. An extraordinary general meeting of shareholders may be convened as
often as necessary. One or more shareholders representing at least 5% of the share capital
may also request in writing that an extraordinary general meeting of shareholders be
convened, stating the intended purpose.
• The independent auditor may also convene an extraordinary general meeting of shareholders,
if necessary. An extraordinary general meeting of shareholders may likewise be convened by
liquidators and representatives of bondholders in accordance with the provisions of the Code
of Obligations.
• The general meeting of shareholders must be convened by the Board of Directors at least
20 days in advance by placing a notice in the Official Gazette of the Republic and Geneva
Canton (Feuille d’avis officielle de la République et Canton de Genève) and in the Swiss
Official Gazette of Commerce.
•

6.4 Agenda items

One or more shareholders representing at least 0.5% of the share capital may request in writing
to add an item to the agenda, specifying their motion; this request must be received at least
60 days prior to the general meeting of shareholders. Under these same conditions, shareholders may also request that motions concerning the items added to the agenda be included
in the invitation to the general meeting of shareholders.

10. Trading lock-up periods
The Head of the Finance Division imposes a lock-up period while the Bank’s results are being
prepared and published. This lock-up period starts no later than two weeks before the close of
the annual or half-year accounting period and ends when the results are published.

The lock-up period applies to the following persons:

Members of the Executive Board
Members of the Board of Directors and member of the Audit Committee appointed by the
State Council
• Employees of the Accounting, Management Control and ALM Departments
• Employees of the Risk Unit
• Employees of Internal Audit
• Director of the Financial Markets and Treasury Department, as well as employees in charge
of the Bank’s Treasury and market making in the Bank’s shares;
• Employees with access to the Executive Board’s SharePoint and those with access to the
Finnova accounting module
• All persons designated by name by the Head of the Finance Division.
•
•

similar to selling or purchasing the Bank’s shares
• revealing confidential information in any manner whatsoever to other Bank employees or to
third parties

Group consolidation process
• Basis of consolidation
When an equity interest is acquired, the difference between the carrying amount of the investment in the consolidated entity and the Group’s share of that entity’s net assets is allocated to
fair value adjustments or to goodwill. Equity is consolidated using the acquisition method: the
purchase price of the equity interest is offset against the Group’s share of the revalued equity
(fair value of net assets) at the acquisition date.
• Goodwill
Goodwill, including goodwill arising on initial consolidation, is carried on the balance sheet under
“Intangible assets” and amortised through the income statement over a maximum period of ten
years. The Group reviews the value of its subsidiaries at the close of each quarter. If the goodwill
value of a subsidiary has suffered an impairment, the carrying amount of the subsidiary is revalued and the corresponding impairment loss is recorded under “Depreciation and amortisation
of fixed assets and impairment on equity investments.”
• Intercompany transactions
Liabilities and receivables as well as income and expenses from intercompany transactions
between consolidated entities are eliminated.
Treatment of foreign currency translation differences
Assets, liabilities, and depreciation of tangible fixed assets of entities domiciled outside
Switzerland and denominated in foreign currencies are translated into CHF at the closing
exchange rates prevailing at the reporting date. Income statement items of these entities, other
than depreciation of tangible fixed assets, are converted into CHF at the average exchange rates
based on the closing rates over the 12 months of the financial year. Equity of these entities is
translated at the historical average exchange rates of each balance-sheet item. The resulting
translation differences are recognised directly in equity under “Currency translation reserve.”
Group valuation methods
The Group’s consolidated financial statements are based on the statutory financial statements
of the Group entities, prepared using uniform accounting principles and valuation methods.
Specific adjustments are made to ensure a true and fair view, primarily with respect to treasury
shares and receivables, which are offset against the corresponding liabilities.

Provisions include amounts intended to cover operating risks, value adjustments for inherent

In addition to specific value adjustments, the Group, as a category 3 institution, makes value
adjustments for inherent default risks, in accordance with Article 25, paragraph 1(b), of
FINMA-AO. Unlike the impaired exposures described above, performing receivables involve
counterparties that are likely to meet their future obligations. The method for calculating value
adjustments for inherent default risk was established by the Group in 2019 and consists of
grouping risk-weighted assets1 by business segment, taking into account both on- and off-balance-sheet exposures.
Eight business segments2 have been identified, with a loss factor calculated for each segment.
The loss factors reflect the estimated loss over a medium-term horizon and are reassessed
annually based on the Group’s historical credit losses, as part of the financial plan review. If the
estimated loss potential appears to be too low or otherwise unsuitable, the estimate may be
adjusted based on an expert assessment. In 2025, this procedure led to a CHF 21.7 million
increase in the expected loss for real estate and corporate banking in France.
The value adjustment for inherent default risk is calculated for each business segment by multiplying the segment’s risk-weighted assets by its loss factor. The combined amounts for these
eight segments constitute the Group’s total value adjustments for inherent risks. The riskweighted assets for each segment are recalculated at each reporting date and the
corresponding value adjustments updated accordingly.

3. Risk management
Continuous improvement of the risk management system
The Group’s risk management system is subject to regular monitoring and review. This ensures
continuous improvement to achieve the system’s stated objectives, specifically:
• identify and analyse the Group’s main risks
• draw lessons from past experience in order to improve overall risk control within the
organisation
• address emerging risks and external risk factors, including regulatory changes.
The units in charge of risk monitoring collectively track and model these risks so that the Group

2. Credit risk
Credit risk relates to the inability of a counterparty or borrower to comply with the terms of a
contract agreed with the Group or to fulfil the obligations outlined therein, affecting the Group’s
profits or capital. Credit risk is inherent in all businesses that depend on the performance of a
counterparty, issuer or borrower. Credit risk also includes counterparty risk in the trading book
and settlement risk.
Credit risk management strategy
The Group’s credit risk management strategy is structured around three pillars:
• Default risk (ratings): the Group regularly analyses the output of rating models, looks at how
internal ratings are used and examines how ratings are allocated. Ratings are generally well
distributed overall, with the exception of the tail ends.
• Risk of decline in the value of collateral: the Group performs quarterly reviews of the real estate
market, which may result in changes in the rules or methodology for granting loans (e.g. a
change in the capitalisation rate). The Group’s trade finance business tracks commodities
prices, while the value of collateral used for secured loans is subject to daily monitoring.
• Loss given default: the Group closely monitors its loan exposures as well as the concentration
of loans. The Group’s risk appetite is applied using a comprehensive system of thresholds and
limits, including country limits and strategic limits.

an aggregate level. The economic value of all underlying positions sensitive to interest rates held
in banking book is evaluated under two antagonistic scenarios (a +/-100 bp parallel shift in interest rates) to determine its sensitivity. The economic value of all strategic hedging transactions
using derivative financial instruments is likewise evaluated under two scenarios (a +/-100 bp of
parallel shift in interest rates).
A hedging transaction is deemed effective for each scenario if:
• the change in the economic value of the underlying position is opposite in sign to the change
in the economic value of the hedging transactions (negative economic correlation); and
• the sensitivity of the hedging transactions is lower in absolute value than the change in the
economic value of the underlying position.
Hedging effectiveness is evaluated at the end of each accounting period (month-end) and the
results are presented to the Bank’s Asset and Liability Management Committee.
Absence of effectiveness of hedging with derivative financial instruments
When hedges with derivative financial instruments are deemed to be no longer effective, the
Group either restores their effectiveness by making a change to the strategic hedging portfolio
with derivative financial instruments, or identifies the specific hedging transactions preventing
them from being effective, and transfers them and records them in the trading book. The excess
gain or loss from hedging transactions with derivative instruments is allocated to “Net trading
income and fair-value adjustments”. The Group has implemented a monitoring system to help
ensure that hedges with derivative instruments are effective across the board, with additional
monitoring prior to the end of each half-year.
